# Research 010 — coupled Laurent monomials
Status: rigorous conditional reduction; not an unconditional RH proof.
Let t=log x, U_k=P_k/(k!x), R_k=U_(k+1)/U_k, T_k=U_k^2/(U_(k-1)U_(k+1)). Then T_k=R_(k-1)/R_k and R_j=R_0/product_{k=1}^j T_k. All positive, tend to 1 by PNT.
Termwise differentiation (locally uniform exponential domination) gives dP_k/dt=P_(k+1)-k P_k. Set r=R_0=P_1/P_0, T=T_1=2P_1^2/(P_0 P_2). Then r'=r^2(2/T-1)-r. Set v=1/r-1. Exactly v'=v+2(1-1/T). Since v tends to zero, integration with terminal condition gives v(t)=-2 integral_t^infty exp(t-u)(1-1/T(u))du.
If T-1=O_epsilon(exp((-1/2+epsilon)t)) for each 0<epsilon<1/2, then v and r-1 have the same bound. Since d log U_0/dt=r-1 and U_0 tends to 1, integrate from t to infinity to obtain log U_0 and U_0-1 with the same bound. Thus P_0(x)-x=O_epsilon(x^(1/2+epsilon)).
For Re(s)>1, integral_0^infty P_0(x)x^(-s-1)dx=-Gamma(s) zeta'(s)/zeta(s), by absolute convergence. Split at 1 and subtract x on [1,infty). The small-x integral is entire by exponential decay; the large-x error integral is holomorphic on Re(s)>1/2 under the bound. The remaining term 1/(s-1) has no other poles. Gamma has no zeros there, so meromorphic uniqueness excludes zeta zeros in Re(s)>1/2; functional equation completes RH. Conversely RH implies P_k=k!x+O_epsilon(x^(1/2+epsilon)) for each fixed k by partial summation and the classical Chebyshev bound, hence the T criterion.
Exact identity T-1=(2P_1^2-P_0 P_2)/(P_0 P_2). PNT gives denominator asymptotic 2x^2. Thus RH is equivalent to |2P_1^2-P_0 P_2|=O_epsilon(x^(3/2+epsilon)). This is a two-monomial polynomial criterion, not a proven estimate. Ordinary Cauchy-Schwarz gives only P_1^2<=P_0 P_2 and does not prove it.
